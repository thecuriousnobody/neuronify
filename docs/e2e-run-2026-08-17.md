## Findings

### Finding 1 — stale quick-reply chips survive a category switch (D1 fail)
After switching Pothole → Graffiti via "Not right? Change it", the three pothole
road-position chips (**Travel lane / Curb or edge / Intersection**) were still
rendered under a graffiti conversation, attached to no pending question. D1's
stated fail condition is "chips linger on a question they don't answer". A
resident tapping one sends "Travel lane" into a graffiti report.

### Finding 5 — an ambiguous intersection is silently resolved to the wrong corner (found by Rajeev, 2026-08-18)

Peoria has **two** Knoxville/Frye intersections, **~5.8 miles apart**:
`N Frye Rd & Knoxville Ave, 61615` (lat 40.796) and
`Knoxville Ave & E Frye Ave, 61604` (lat 40.712).

Probing `/api/v2/geocode` on localhost shows the result is **order- and
spelling-sensitive**:

| query | candidates returned |
|---|---|
| `Knoxville and Frye` | **2 — `N Frye Rd` FIRST**, `E Frye Ave` second |
| `Frye and Knoxville` | 1 — `E Frye Ave` |
| `Fry and Knoxville` | 1 — `E Frye Ave` |
| `Knoxville and North Frye` | 1 — `N Frye Rd` |

`pickCandidates` preserves provider order and `geocodeApprox` takes `[0]`, so the
selection logic is doing what it says. The defect is upstream: **when the phrase
reaching the geocoder is a reordered or re-spelled version of what the resident
said, two candidates collapse to one — and the "Not this spot?" alternates row
never renders, because there are no longer any alternates.** The resident is
never offered the corner they meant. This is [[Finding 3]] (the agent rewrites
the resident's words) with a dispatch consequence rather than a cosmetic one.

**Open question that decides the fix:** whether the geocoded string is the
resident's verbatim phrase or the agent's extraction. Rajeev to confirm what the
"Where is it?" field read on his report.

**Candidate fixes:**
1. Geocode the **verbatim** resident phrase, not the extracted value — restores
   the ambiguity so the alternates row does its job.
2. When the top two candidates are more than ~1 mile apart, **ask** rather than
   auto-pin. Distance is the available signal that a guess is dangerous.

**Workaround that works today:** typing `Knoxville and North Frye` into "Where is
it?" resolves unambiguously; F2 and F4 prove the pin and the filed record both
follow the field.

### Two near-misses I did NOT log as bugs (method honesty)
1. Early on, a typed message "vanished" and the report appeared not to send. That
   looked exactly like the known "composer drops first input after page load"
   polish item. It was **not** — `document.visibilityState` was `hidden`, so the
   synthetic keystrokes never reached the page. Re-tested via `form_input`; the
   app was fine.
2. The desk passcode eye toggle appeared dead to a ref-click. A real DOM `.click()`
   flipped `type=password`→`text` and the label to "Hide passcode". App fine.

Both would have been false alarms of exactly the kind that cost real time on
2026-08-10. Verify the harness before blaming the app.

### Finding 3 — the agent rewrites the resident's words into the record
My opener was `TEST — there's a big pothole at Fry and Knoxville, right in the
traffic lane, about the size of a dinner plate`. The description stored on the
case is `pothole in the traffic lane` — the agent's paraphrase, with the
`TEST —` marker stripped. Two consequences:
1. **Operational:** filed test records are no longer identifiable by the `TEST —`
   convention the ground rules depend on. Anyone cleaning the queue by searching
   `TEST` will miss them. This is why ref numbers had to carry the ledger.
2. **Substantive:** the record is the city's account of what the resident said,
   and it is not what they said. Same item flagged 2026-07-31 ("agent paraphrases
   resident's words"); still present. The `[edited]` suffix I added on the review
   screen did survive, so resident edits are respected — it is the *initial*
   extraction that rewrites.

### Finding 4 — a typed chat is labelled "voice" on the staff record
The desk detail header reads `Peoria, IL · voice · submitted Aug 17, 1:55 PM`
and the transcript panel is titled **RESIDENT'S INTAKE (VOICE)**. This report
was typed, never spoken. Low severity, but it is staff-visible provenance on an
append-only record, and "voice" vs "typed" is exactly the sort of thing that
matters if a case is ever disputed.
